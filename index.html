The Problem

AI automates the analysis.
But who designs the study?

The decisions that determine what a study can discover are made before any analysis begins — and they are usually made implicitly.

🔍

A mis-specified model can't be rescued

No statistical method fixes a study that asked the wrong question or missed a confounder. Design errors are permanent — they bound what the study can find.

🧠

Expertise is siloed

Real problems span disciplines. No individual commands the breadth to name every relevant mechanism across entomology, hydrology, remote sensing and epidemiology at once.

📋

Design decisions go unrecorded

Months of literature review and scoping collapse into a methods paragraph. What was considered and rejected — the roads not taken — is almost never written down.

The Approach

Three projections, each one lossy
— and each one recorded

A research design is a series of narrowings from the world to what you can measure. ResearchArchitect makes each narrowing explicit instead of silent.

From a question to a design you can defend

You supply the research question, your data, and the scope of the study. The system proposes the influence network; you correct it. Every variable, every link, and every judgement about what falls inside or outside the study is something you can point at, disagree with, and change.

What you end up with is a design that knows its own boundaries: which relationships matter most, which are observable within your window, and which were deliberately set aside.

How it works, step by step →
1 Research question — broad, cross-domain exploration
↓
2 Influence network — proposed, then refined by you
↓
3 Edge importance — necessary, driving, or modifying
↓
4 Scope — what can vary in your window and extent
↓
5 Data coverage — what your data can actually measure
↓
6 Hypotheses — testable, and honest about their limits
